# NEEDS CHECKING

Questions deliberately **kept out of the bank** because the fact behind them is not
settled enough to enshrine. A wrong fact survives every playtest — nobody in the room
knows either — so these wait here until somebody confirms them.

Confirm one, write the entry, merge it, delete the line.

---

## From batch 1 (desh — food & drink)

- **Mango capital / Fazli.** Is Chapainawabganj *the* mango district, or does Rajshahi
  have the better claim? Also whether Fazli is the variety with GI status attached to
  which district. Both are commonly asserted and commonly contradicted. Written around
  for now — the bank has a Dinajpur litchi question instead.
- **Mango ripening order.** Gopalbhog early → Khirsapat → Langra → Fazli → Ashwina
  latest. A lovely question if the order is right; not confident enough about the tail.
- **Haji biryani, Old Dhaka.** Every detail about it is asserted somewhere and denied
  somewhere else — no potato, mustard oil, eaten with the hand. Skipped entirely.
- **Gachi.** The word for the men who tap date palms for sap in winter. Almost certainly
  right; would make a good question on its own if confirmed.

## From batch 10 (probash — USA)

- **Sheikh Rahman, Georgia.** Reported as the first Bangladeshi-American elected to a US
  state senate, in 2018. Would be the batch's Atlanta hook. ~75% confident.
- **Paterson, New Jersey.** There is a Bangladeshi community there and possibly a street
  co-named for Bangladesh. Neither detail confirmed.
- **"Bangladesh Street", Kensington, Brooklyn.** A co-naming around 2022 on McDonald or
  Church Avenue. Recalled, not verified.
- **Bangladesh Society of New York**, founded 1975 and described as the oldest
  Bangladeshi organisation in the US. Plausible, unverified.

## From batch 8 (probash — UK, East London)

- **Janomot**, described as the oldest Bengali-language newspaper in Britain, published
  from the East End since 1969. Would make a good question; the founding date and the
  "oldest" claim both need a source.
- **East London Mosque and the adhan.** Reported as one of the first mosques in Britain
  permitted to broadcast the call to prayer aloud. ~70% confident, left out.
- **The Sylheti sending districts.** Biswanath, Balaganj, Golapganj and Beanibazar are
  named everywhere as the origin of most British Bangladeshis, but "above all which
  one" is exactly the kind of detail that a question would have to get right.
- **Boishakhi Mela route.** Ends at Weavers Fields — recalled, not verified.

## From the desh batches

- **Bangladesh's highest peak** — genuinely contested (Saka Haphong, Tazing Dong and
  others, with no settled survey). The prompt names this one as a skip and it stayed
  skipped.
- **The tallest waterfall** — same problem: Madhabkunda and Nafakhum both claimed.
- **Nappi / sidol.** The Hill Tracts fermented fish paste is in the bank; the exact
  relationship between nappi, sidol and shidol across the hills and Sylhet is
  asserted loosely in the `why` and could be tightened by somebody who cooks it.
- **Niaz Murshed as South Asia's first grandmaster** (1987, ahead of Anand in 1988).
  In the bank, framed so the answer is the sport rather than the claim, but the claim
  is in the `why` and is worth one check.

## Policy judgement, not accuracy

- **The Concert for Bangladesh** (Madison Square Garden, 1971). Great trivia, and the
  framing is a benefit concert rather than the war itself — but it is inseparable from
  1971, which the deck refuses on purpose. Left out; worth a decision rather than a
  quiet inclusion.
- **`pr-altab-ali-park` is IN the bank** — flagged here so the call is visible rather
  than buried. Altab Ali Park is central to British Bangladeshi identity and the
  question itself asks for a park name, so the lies players write are park names. The
  `why` states plainly that the park is named for a young man killed in a racist attack
  in 1978. That is history told soberly, not atrocity offered as bluffing fodder — but
  it is the closest anything in the bank comes to the line, so it is worth a second
  opinion. Removing it costs the bank one question and nothing else.
- **`pr-libya-route` is IN the bank** — the sea crossing to Italy. The question asks
  which country migrants set out from and the `why` is deliberately flat, but the
  subject carries drowned people behind it. Same call as above: defensible, and worth
  making on purpose rather than by omission.
- **Rana Plaza and the 1943 famine were both left out** of the modern-life and history
  batches. Neither is on the linter's blocked list and both are legitimate history;
  both are also mass death, which is the thing the deck refuses to hand players as
  bluffing fodder. Noted so the absence reads as a decision.
